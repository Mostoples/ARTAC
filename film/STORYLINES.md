# ARTA-C: three documentary storylines (I2ASPO, English narration, 75-90 s, 16:9 1080p)

## 1. "Day 47" (recommended: a stranger follows one person's day and remembers it)

**Device / form:** a moment in someone's day; one continuous camera take through one home.

The camera never cuts. It drifts from dawn light on a kitchen table, through the ARTA-C device, inside its mechanism, and back out to the PMO's phone across town. The viewer lives one ordinary morning, day 47 of a 180-day TB treatment, and feels how fragile "every single day" is, and how the machine quietly holds it together.

- **First 2 s:** 06:59 on the device screen in warm dawn light. Beside it, a blister of red caplets. Narrator: *"Tuberculosis is curable."*
- **Beats:**
  1. hook: "…if you finish the treatment"
  2. tension: 180 days, a supervisor who cannot be there every morning
  3. reveal: ARTA-C wakes
  4. proof (face check → finger PPG → the toaster mechanism lowers a row → pecks one caplet → it rolls into the tray)
  5. payoff: the PMO's phone lights up "Taken ✓" miles away
  6. claim: "No dose goes unseen"
  7. end card
- **Look:** warm-to-cool morning light on a real table set; AQUENT white-blue device; the 3D UI glowing on screen. Ghost-transparent casing only when we go inside.
- **Sound:** soft felt piano and room tone, real mechanical foley (servo whirr, foil pop, caplet click), one bass swell at the payoff.

## 2. "Inside the Machine"

**Device / form:** the object is the explanation; an endless zoom.

Each shot dives into a detail that becomes the next world: the screen's heart icon becomes the finger tunnel, the tunnel becomes the blister dome, the dome becomes the gear teeth, the gears become data rising into the cloud. It is engineering as wonder, for a jury that wants to see how it works.

- **First 2 s:** extreme macro of a single red caplet inside a foil dome, the plunger tip entering frame. Narrator: *"This is one dose."*
- **Beats:**
  1. hook macro
  2. pull back to reveal the whole blister
  3. dive in through the screen, then the camera, PPG, gears, plunger and chute
  4. payoff: the caplet falls in slow motion
  5. claim
  6. end card
- **Look:** clean white studio cyclorama, exploded views with technical labels, blueprint line overlays fading into the 3D render.
- **Sound:** cinematic pulse building on servo rhythms, a sub-hit for every dive.

## 3. "180" (the bold one)

**Device / form:** a number lands; type carries the argument.

The film is a wall of 180 small pills, one per day of treatment, filling the screen. Missed days crack and fall out; the wall collapses. ARTA-C rebuilds it pill by pill. The device and its mechanism appear only inside that counting.

- **First 2 s:** giant kinetic type "180", each digit made of red caplets. Narrator: *"One hundred and eighty mornings."*
- **Beats:**
  1. hook
  2. days tick by
  3. a missed day cracks the wall
  4. reveal ARTA-C
  5. proof inside the counter
  6. payoff: the full 180 completes
  7. claim
  8. end card
- **Look:** bold white-blue typographic grid, red caplets as the only warm colour, hard cuts on the beat.
- **Sound:** a ticking clock motif turning into a driving beat.

**Assumptions (all storylines):**
- Length 75–90 s, 1920×1080 at 30 fps.
- English TTS narrator: free neural voice via edge-tts if available, otherwise offline Windows voice.
- Music generated locally (royalty-free).
- No real faces: people appear as hands and silhouettes, so there are no likeness or consent issues.
- Any statistic (e.g. WHO TB burden) is used only with its source shown on screen.
